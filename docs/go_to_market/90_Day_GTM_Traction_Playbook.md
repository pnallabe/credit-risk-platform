## Strategic Frame: One Principle Before Everything Else

You are not selling software. You are selling **regulatory certainty with a 30-day proof window**. Every tactic below flows from that frame. The moment a CRO, Chief Compliance Officer, or Model Risk Manager engages, the core message is: *"You are 90 days from your next exam cycle. We eliminate the part that keeps you up at night — and we prove it in 30 days, risk-free."*

### Solopreneur Operating Rule

**Never spend time on a task that has a Claude prompt.** If you find yourself writing a first draft of anything — an email, a slide, a document — stop and write the prompt instead. Your job is judgment and relationships. AI's job is everything else. Every section below includes explicit callouts for what AI handles end-to-end vs. what only you can do.

## The Two Asymmetric Leverage Moves (Start Here)

Before the 90-day plan, internalize these two moves. They have 10x the return-on-effort of everything else combined.

### Leverage Move #1: Manufacture an SR 11-7 Inspection "Trigger Event"

Right now, regulators are actively citing mid-market lenders for MRM deficiencies — FDIC exam findings from 2025 are publicly available in enforcement action databases. **Build a single-page "SR 11-7 Gap Scanner" — a free, ungated, 5-minute self-assessment tool** (Google Form or lightweight webapp) that lets a model risk manager rate their institution across the 12 SR 11-7 control domains. Deliver a scored PDF output with a gap heatmap. Gate only one thing: the remediation guidance requires a 20-minute call.

This converts thought leadership into a **qualified pipeline generator with built-in urgency**. You get a warm, pre-qualified lead who has self-diagnosed a problem you solve. As a solopreneur, this asset works while you sleep — your highest-ROI build in Week 1.

**Solopreneur build breakdown (~6 hours total, AI does 90%):**
- Use Claude to draft the 12-domain SR 11-7 self-assessment questions
- Use Typeform or Tally.so (free) for the form — no code required
- Use Claude to generate the scored PDF output template with gap heatmap language
- Use Canva for the PDF design
- **Your time:** Review + approve at each step. Nothing to write from scratch.

### Leverage Move #2: Land One Credit Union League as a Channel Partner

There are 34 state credit union leagues affiliated with CUNA/America's Credit Unions. Each league hosts quarterly compliance roundtables and has direct email access to 50–500 member credit unions. **One MOU with a single league — even a small one like the Michigan Credit Union League or Georgia Credit Union Affiliates — immediately gives you 100–300 warm introductions** framed as a member benefit, not a cold pitch. The ask to the league is not a paid partnership. The ask is: "Let us present a 45-minute SR 11-7 readiness session to your members at your next compliance roundtable. Free. We will make your compliance team look good." This is Week 2–3 priority.

**Solopreneur execution (~3 hours prep + one 30-minute call):**
- Use Perplexity to research the 5 most active state CU leagues and their compliance event calendars
- Use Claude to draft the outreach email and speaking proposal
- Use Claude to build the 45-minute SR 11-7 session slide deck
- **Your time:** One human relationship-building call to close it. This is your highest-leverage single human action in 90 days.

## 1. Beachhead Customer Acquisition

### Who to Target (Buyer Sequencing)

Do not try to close all three stakeholders simultaneously in the first 90 days. Sequence matters:

| Priority | Role | Why First |
|---|---|---|
| **1 (Primary)** | Chief Risk Officer / VP Credit Risk | Economic buyer, owns the SR 11-7 and CECL mandate, budget authority |
| **2 (Champion)** | Model Risk Manager / Model Validation Lead | Daily user, technical champion, feels the pain acutely, will drive internal urgency |
| **3 (Enabler)** | Chief Compliance Officer | CFPB fair lending angle; co-signs the contract but rarely initiates |

The VP of IT/Engineering is a **blocker, not a buyer**. Engage them only after the CRO is sold. For credit unions under $500M AUM, the CRO and CCO are often the same person.

### Which Channels (Ranked by Expected Conversion Speed)

**Channel 1: LinkedIn Direct Outreach (Weeks 1–4)**

Target Model Risk Managers and CROs at credit unions with $200M–$2B in assets. Use LinkedIn Sales Navigator. Build a list of 300 names. Do not pitch the product. The outreach sequence:

- *Day 0:* Comment substantively on a post they made about exam prep, CECL, or model governance. No ask.
- *Day 3:* Connection request with a 1-line note: "I read your post on [CECL/SR 11-7] — working on a problem adjacent to yours. Thought it worth connecting."
- *Day 7 (after acceptance):* Send the SR 11-7 Gap Scanner link with one sentence: "Built this for MRMs dealing with exam prep cycles — no pitch, just signal. Curious what your gaps look like."
- *Day 14 (if they use the scanner):* "Your gap score is in the [red/amber] range on model documentation and ongoing monitoring — these are the exact items FDIC cited in [specific enforcement action]. Happy to share what we built to address this."

Expected conversion: 3–5% of 300 = 9–15 discovery calls from this channel alone in 30 days.

**Solopreneur execution (30 min/day, AI-scaled):**
- Use Clay or Apollo to build the 300-name prospect list automatically — define criteria once
- Use Claude to write the 4-message outreach sequence once, then personalize only the first line per person (30 seconds each)
- Use LinkedIn's native scheduler or Taplio to queue 3x/week posts
- Post formula: 60% tactical/educational (SR 11-7, CECL, CFPB enforcement), 30% product validation (anonymized pilot results), 10% direct offer (pilot invite, scanner link)
- **Your time:** 30 min/day. No virtual assistant needed.

**Channel 2: RegTech Consultant Referral Network (Weeks 2–6)**

Independent model validation consultants and compliance advisory firms are the **fastest path to warm pipeline**. They already have trust relationships with your exact buyers. Target firms like Protiviti Financial Services, Crowe LLP (credit union practice), Wipfli CU advisory team, and boutique MRM shops. The offer: a white-label integration where their consultants recommend ILOL as implementation infrastructure, and you share a 10–15% referral fee or give them client-facing reporting capabilities for free. Three referral partner relationships closed in the first 60 days can generate more qualified pipeline than 6 months of direct outreach.

**Solopreneur execution (~2 hours setup, then 20-min calls):**
- Use LinkedIn to identify 20 Wipfli/Crowe/Moss Adams partners with CU/community bank practices
- Use Claude to write the referral partner deck (2 slides: the problem, the offer)
- Use Calendly embedded in one email to reduce scheduling friction to zero
- **Your time:** 10 conversations → 2 agreements. Each conversation is 20 minutes.

**Channel 3: Community Bank / CU Conference Speaking (Weeks 4–12)**

Submit a speaking proposal immediately to: CUNA GAC (October 2026), NAFCU Annual Conference (July 2026), and America's Credit Unions Innovation Summit. Lead time for speaker selection is 60–90 days, so submit now. Title the talk: *"What SR 11-7 Examiners Are Actually Looking For in 2026: A Real-Time Model Risk Readiness Assessment."* This is not a product pitch — it is a working session. Product demo happens at the back of the room.

**Channel 4: The Thought-Leadership Article (Conversion, not awareness)**

The article is an asset, not a campaign. Do not boost it with paid spend. Instead: re-purpose it as a **personalized outreach artifact**. ## 2. Proof-of-Value: Structuring the 30-Day Pilot

### Pre-Pilot: The Critical Setup Conversation

The most common reason pilots fail to convert is that success was never defined upfront. Before the pilot starts, run a 60-minute "Pilot Scoping Call" that locks in writing:

1. **Which 3 SR 11-7 control domains will ILOL be evaluated against?**
2. **What does "good" look like at Day 30 for the Model Risk Manager? For the CRO?**
3. **Who is the internal sponsor who will present pilot results to the executive team?**
4. **What is the current baseline being compared against?** (Hours to generate exam documentation, manual steps in model monitoring workflow, etc.)

Do not proceed to a pilot without answers to all four.

> **Solopreneur cap: Maximum 2 simultaneous pilots.** Quality of pilot execution is your only moat right now. Do not sacrifice delivery depth for pipeline breadth.

**Solopreneur pilot toolkit (AI-generated, you review):**
- Use Claude to generate the Pilot Scoping Call agenda and success metric template
- Use Notion AI or Coda to build a lightweight pilot tracker shared with the customer
- Use Claude to draft the Week 4 exam packet simulation output — you customize, not write from scratch
- **Your time:** ~5 hours/week per active pilot. Two pilots = 10 hours/week maximum commitment.

### The 30-Day Pilot Structure

| Week | Focus | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Data integration + baseline audit | ILOL connected to their core system or loan data export; baseline gap report generated |
| **Week 2** | Live decisioning + compliance run | At least one policy rule live; one HMDA/fair lending analysis completed against their actual portfolio |
| **Week 3** | Model documentation sprint | Auto-generate model card for their primary credit model; compare against SR 11-7 documentation requirements |
| **Week 4** | Exam packet simulation | Generate one mock regulatory exam response packet; walk CRO and compliance team through it in a 90-minute session |

### Success Metrics to Lock Upfront

| Metric | Baseline Target | ILOL Target |
|---|---|---|
| Time to generate exam-ready model documentation | 3–5 weeks (manual) | < 4 hours |
| Fair lending disparate impact analysis turnaround | 2–4 weeks | < 24 hours |
| SR 11-7 control coverage (documented) | < 60% | > 85% |
| Portfolio anomaly detection to alert time | 30–90 days | < 72 hours |

These are conservative and achievable. The pilot is designed to **over-deliver on metrics the buyer already cares about**, not to showcase features they haven't asked for.

### Converting Pilots to Case Studies

On Day 25 (before the pilot ends), propose the case study to your internal champion: "We want to write a 2-page case study documenting what you achieved. You control the final content and approval. We will co-present it at [CUNA compliance roundtable / RegTech newsletter]. This costs you nothing and gives your compliance team external recognition." Most of your pilots will come from institutions whose teams are under-resourced and under-recognized. A co-branded case study is both valuable and flattering.

## 6. Regulatory Tailwind Positioning

### Using Enforcement Actions as Outreach Triggers

The FFIEC maintains a public database of enforcement actions. The CFPB publishes supervisory highlights quarterly. These are **live trigger events** for outreach.

**Template outreach trigger:** When a new enforcement action cites MRM deficiencies or fair lending violations at a peer institution, send a 3-sentence message to CROs at similar-sized institutions the same week:

> "The [Regulator] just cited [Peer Institution] for [specific MRM/fair lending deficiency]. This is the exact gap ILOL was built to close — and we can run a diagnostic on your institution's exposure in 48 hours. Would a 20-minute call be worth it?"

This is not fear-mongering. It is timely relevance. A CRO who sees a peer cited is already thinking about their own exposure. Your outreach converts ambient anxiety into a conversation.

### The Three Regulatory Clocks to Reference in Every Outreach

1. **SR 11-7 Model Validation Cycle:** Most mid-market lenders are 12–24 months behind on required ongoing model monitoring. This is not theoretical — examiners are citing it actively in 2025–2026.
2. **CECL Adoption Deadline:** Institutions that adopted CECL in 2023 are now in their second full annual cycle. Auditors are scrutinizing the quality of their ACL models — and most MRM infrastructure at mid-market shops cannot support the documentation requirements.
3. **CFPB HMDA/Fair Lending Annual Filing (March each year):** The weeks immediately after HMDA filing (March–April) are when compliance officers realize their data quality and disparate impact analysis gaps. **Right now (April 2026) is peak outreach season** for the fair lending angle.

## Priority Stack for a Solopreneur (AI-Augmented)

### The Core Constraint
Every hour spent on tasks AI can do is an hour stolen from the two things only you can do: **relationship-building and closing**. This stack is organized around that principle.

### The Five Priorities, in Execution Order

**Priority 1 — Build the SR 11-7 Gap Scanner (Days 1–5)**
~6 hours total. AI does 90%. Once live, this asset generates qualified leads continuously with zero additional effort from you.

**Priority 2 — LinkedIn Outreach Engine (Days 3–90, 30 min/day)**
Clay/Apollo builds the list. Claude writes the sequences. You personalize the first line only. Taplio queues the posts. You show up for the conversations that result.

**Priority 3 — One League Relationship (Weeks 2–3, one focused push)**
This is a human task — AI cannot do it. But AI fully prepares you (research, email, slide deck). Your job is one 30-minute call. This is your highest-leverage single human action in 90 days.

**Priority 4 — Two Compliance Consultancy Referral Agreements (Weeks 3–6)**
Claude writes the pitch deck and outreach. Calendly eliminates scheduling friction. Your job: 10 conversations, 20 minutes each → 2 agreements. These two agreements become your fastest path to warm, qualified introductions.

**Priority 5 — Two Pilots, Metrics-First (Weeks 4–8)**
Cap at 2 simultaneous pilots. Claude generates all documentation scaffolding. You deliver the relationship, the judgment, and the Day 4 exam packet walkthrough. Two pilots = ~10 hours/week. Everything downstream — case studies, references, fundraising — depends on pilot quality, not pilot quantity.

### What AI Handles End-to-End (Do Not Touch These Yourself)

| Task | AI Tool | Your Involvement |
|---|---|---|
| First-draft outreach sequences | Claude | Review + approve |
| Speaking proposal + slide decks | Claude + Canva | Light edit |
